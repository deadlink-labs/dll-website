4. WHAT CAN GO WRONG WITH THE TOOL ITSELF

  a) Data. The indicator computes on whatever data feed TradingView serves
     for the chart you are looking at. Different exchanges, different
     results: the same asset can flip weeks apart on two venues. Feed
     errors, missing bars, delistings and exchange outages all change what
     is drawn.
  b) Parameters. The per-asset table is a calibration as of the date on this
     file. It can be wrong for an asset, it can stop being right as markets
     change, and it has not been measured on timeframes other than the
     weekly. The default pair applied to unlisted assets is a best guess.
  c) Warm-up. On symbols with short history the calculation has not settled
     and the readings are rough. The card says so; believe it.
  d) Alerts. Alerts on indicators are a TradingView feature, available on
     their paid plans only, and delivered by their systems, which can be
     delayed or fail. An alert is frozen on the script version it was
     created with. Never rely on an alert as your only line of defence.
  e) Platform changes. TradingView can change Pine Script, its data or its
     terms at any time in ways that break or alter this indicator.
  f) Bugs. This is software. It has bugs nobody has found yet.
